# news-summary-01 — summary of 11 November 2026 world news

Entry for the make.fun bounty "Write the best summary of 11 November's world
news". The program writes one summary of the world's news of Wednesday,
11 November 2026, in English, to `/output/entry.txt`.

## How it works

`main.py` holds the summary as a fixed string and writes it out. No network,
no clock, no randomness: every run produces the exact same bytes.

```sh
python3 main.py   # writes /output/entry.txt
```

## The summary

Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Iran dominated the day. U.S. Secretary of
State Marco Rubio said in Paris that America's economic campaign against
Tehran is working so well that 'there's really no need for major combat
operations any longer,' though force remains an option if Iran crosses red
lines. Tehran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Iran is still waiting for
a U.S. answer to its seven conditions for talks, and the Strait of Hormuz
stays closed until they are met. Three oil tankers caught fire after hitting
naval mines, and monitors counted at least 37 attacks on ships in and around
Hormuz this month, the most since the war began nine months ago.

In Abu Dhabi, the first day of U.S.-mediated talks between Ukraine and Russia
was 'constructive and quite substantive,' President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said,
and the sides agreed a large prisoner-of-war exchange as the talks closed.
NATO scrambled jets after drones strayed into Baltic airspace during
Ukrainian strikes on Russia.

In Washington, the House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment resolution
against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it.
The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20
alleged plots in the U.S. and Europe, including one at Britain's RAF Fairford
air base, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base after
threats to his life. In Gaza, Israeli forces killed at least seven people,
including a father and his 13-year-old son.

World leaders gathered in Antalya for the COP31 climate summit, where U.N.
chief Antonio Guterres warned the slow response to climate change has put the
world 'on a path to destruction.' In the Democratic Republic of Congo, Ebola
has now infected 11,042 people and killed at least 5,387, the second-deadliest
outbreak on record. A U.K. court struck down Trinidad and Tobago's
centuries-old ban on gay sex.
